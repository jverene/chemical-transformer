\begin{abstract}
Token-level adaptive compute usually keys off endogenous signals---attention
entropy, router scores, confidence---assumed to track per-token difficulty.
On arithmetic with graded difficulty we show this assumption fails three
ways: an entropy-driven gate collapses to a constant, a tag-initialized
gate inverts, and an unsupervised learned head is vacuous.
Difficulty-supervised targets under a budget constraint do produce ordered
gates, but a pre-registered shuffled-targets control shows the ordering
carries no accuracy benefit: gate-grad targets differ from permuted targets
by 0.0008 held-out loss at 1.4B, and by at most 0.3 accuracy points at 11M.
Measuring the field directly---the per-token gradient of loss with respect
to each token's compute gate---explains why: the field has a life cycle
(real structure mid-training, consumed by training, flat at convergence),
and a causal test shows its structure is not trainable---chasing it online
loses to random allocation of the same budget at every scale tested. The
surviving recipe needs no allocator: token-level FFN dropout, where each
token receives full FFN compute only inside randomly rotating windows. At
1.4B web-text fine-tuning it trails dense by \NlRotGapPct\% held-out loss
(3 seeds) at 50\% billed FFN training FLOPs, inside the pre-registered
2--10\% band; a gather-based realization converts half the billed cost into
a measured 1.35$\times$ training throughput. Failures on code and
template-dominated math are reported, not hidden.
\end{abstract}

% ---- ACTIVE INTRO (dropout-lead; parity-conditional sentence marked) ----
Transformers spend most of their training compute on feed-forward blocks
applied uniformly to every token at every step. We ask a simple question:
does every token need the FFN at every step? The adaptive-compute literature
answers with learned allocators---routers, entropy gates, difficulty
heads---trained end-to-end to switch compute on where it ``matters.'' We
first show this program fails on its own terms in a controlled testbed with
per-token ground truth: endogenous signals collapse, invert, or stay vacuous
(\S\ref{sec:failures}), supervised targets produce correctly ordered gates
whose ordering carries no accuracy benefit, and a pre-registered
shuffled-targets control shows target content is irrelevant at 11M and
150M scale. Figure~\ref{fig:teaser} previews the three findings.
The positive half of the paper is a measurement that explains these
failures and a recipe it motivates. We measure the per-token
\emph{value of compute}---the gradient of the loss with respect to each
token's FFN gate---and find it has a life cycle: real structure
mid-training, consumed by training, flat at convergence (\S\ref{sec:measure}).
An oracle bounds the converged field's headroom, and a causal test shows
that \emph{chasing} the field online is worse than random allocation of the
same budget---at 11M and, replicated, in the starved-150M regime where the
field's mid-life structure is demonstrably present (\S\ref{sec:results}). What works is the degenerate allocator everyone
skips: train with \emph{token-level FFN dropout}---each token receives full
FFN compute only inside randomly rotating windows---and deploy dense. At the
150M convergence pair it trails dense held-out loss by \ConvRotGapPct\% at
exactly half the billed FFN training FLOPs, and by \NlRotGapPct\% on 1.4B
web-text fine-tuning (3 seeds)---claims we scope to compute-limited regimes
per their pre-registered decision rules---and it requires no
labels or routers, and composes with any attention architecture because it
never touches attention.

\paragraph{Contributions.}
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{A three-way failure diagnosis} of endogenous allocation signals
(collapse, inversion, vacuity), each with a mechanistic account
(\S\ref{sec:failures}); a standard Mixture-of-Depths router fails
analogously.
\item \textbf{Compute-value supervision}: targets derived offline from the
trained model itself---per-token loss, and $\partial\mathcal{L}/\partial g$
in one backward pass---match label-supervised allocation, with a
shuffled-targets control (and a pre-registered decision rule) separating
the specific measured targets from supervised variation alone
(\S\ref{sec:measure}, \S\ref{sec:p0}); the value field's per-bin signature
is measured at 42M and 150M, where the pre-registered migration prediction
fails and the converged field is flat (\S\ref{sec:scale}).
\item \textbf{Iso-FLOPs methodology}: all method comparisons at matched
\emph{billed training FLOPs} (gated billing, forward + backward), with
within-segment interpolation and a budget sweep; the ``similar total
budget'' caveat of an earlier workshop version of this work is retired
(\S\ref{sec:isoflops}).
\item \textbf{Scale replication, honestly scoped}: the failure taxonomy,
the shuffled-control content-irrelevance result, and the causal
field-chasing test all replicate at 150M (probe grid, starved regime); the
dropout recipe's parity claim is scoped to compute-limited regimes by its
pre-registered decision rule ($-5.3\%$ loss at $50\%$ billed FFN FLOPs at
150M; $-\NlRotGapPct\%$ at 1.4B web-text fine-tuning; the
template-dominated-data boundary is reported, not hidden)
(\S\ref{sec:scale}, \S\ref{sec:nl}).
\item \textbf{Realization}: a tiered-FFN serving benchmark showing
tokens/sec scaling with the mean gate, converting billed-FLOPs savings into
wall-clock (\S\ref{sec:realization}).
\end{enumerate}

\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{Adaptive computation and early exit.} Adaptive Computation Time
\citep{graves2016act} and its transformer descendant \citep{dehghani2019universal}
learn per-input halting and are known for halting scores collapsing to
constants; our collapse diagnostic is the per-token version of the same
failure, with the same underlying cause---weak, task-loss-only feedback
\citep{bengio2013estimating}. Early-exit and cascades
\citep{teerapittayanon2016branchynet,xin2020deebert,elbayad2020depth,
schuster2022calm} and dynamic routing \citep{wang2018skipnet} key compute
depth to learned confidence signals; the confidence-versus-value gap we
measure (easy tokens carry the largest loss gradients) is a training-time
account of why confidence signals under-serve hard inputs
\citep{guo2017calibration}. Mixture-of-Depths \citep{raposo2024mixture} is
the strongest recent allocator; our MoD baseline inherits a handicap we
state plainly---its router is trained during a short fine-tune rather than a
full pretraining run---so that comparison bounds router-from-scratch at our
budget, not MoD at scale.

\paragraph{Conditional computation and mixture-of-experts routing.} Sparse
MoE \citep{jacobs1991adaptive,shazeer2017outrageously,lepikhin2021gshard,
fedus2022switch} attaches learned routers to expert selection, and router
training is notoriously brittle: load-balancing losses, router z-losses, and
initialization are load-bearing \citep{zoph2022stmoe}, and gradient
estimation through discrete routing decisions is a recognized obstacle
\citep{bengio2013estimating}. Our difficulty heads inherit this feedback
poverty (the vacuity diagnostic). Slimmable networks
\citep{yu2019universally} switch capacity at deployment; our budget-pinned
gates are the per-token analogue.

\paragraph{Regularization as the null allocator.} The surviving recipe is
structured dropout, a lineage we position against explicitly rather than
claim novelty over: elementwise dropout \citep{srivastava2014dropout},
stochastic depth \citep{huang2016stochastic}, LayerDrop's per-layer
structured dropout \citep{fan2019layerdrop}, and random token dropping
during language-model training \citep{fan2021nlpscratch}. The contribution
is not the regularizer but the iso-FLOPs result that surrounds it: at
matched billed compute, every signal-carrying allocator we test is matched
by (or loses to) the random one, and the measured value field that would
justify non-random allocation is not trainable online. Our variant also
differs in granularity (per-token, per-layer FFN windows; attention
untouched) and in deployment (dense inference, never gated).

\paragraph{Saliency and the value of compute.} Eq.~\eqref{eq:gategrad} is a
per-token, per-layer saliency score for \emph{compute} rather than weights,
in the lineage of Optimal Brain Damage \citep{lecun1990obd} and
Taylor-expansion pruning \citep{molchanov2019importance}; to our knowledge
its life cycle---real mid-training, consumed by training, flat at
convergence---has not previously been measured for token-level compute
allocation. The same first-order quantity underlies draft-model
disagreement in speculative decoding
\citep{leviathan2023speculative,chen2023speculative}: acceptance rates
measure ``hard for a small model''; our gate gradients measure ``helpable
by this layer,'' for layers instead of models.

\paragraph{Token importance at inference.} Attention-mass and heavy-hitter
token selection \citep{zhang2023h2o} and token pruning/merging in vision
\citep{rao2021dynamicvit,bolya2023tome} exploit stable importance
structure at inference; our converged-field result (loss-gradient
saliency is coin-flip at convergence) explains why such signals must be
architectural (attention mass), not gradient-based.

\paragraph{Controls and memorization.} The shuffled-targets control follows
the logic of random-label controls \citep{zhang2017rethinking}: if permuted
supervision matches real supervision, the specific content is not what
carries the effect. Curriculum ordering \citep{bengio2009curriculum} is the
supervised-allocation hypothesis our tag arm tests, at token rather than
example granularity.

\section{Method}
\label{sec:method}

\subsection{Architecture and per-token gating}
\label{sec:arch}

Each transformer layer \citep{vaswani2017attention} computes
\begin{equation}
x_2 = \LN\!\bigl(x_1 + \attn(x_1)\bigr), \qquad
x_3 = \LN\!\bigl(x_2 + g_t \cdot \FFN(x_2)\bigr),
\label{eq:gate}
\end{equation}
where $g_t \in [0,1]$ is a per-token, per-layer gate produced by a small
per-layer \emph{DifficultyHead} (a two-layer MLP on the post-attention state
$x_2$, sigmoid-squashed). Compute is billed proportionally: a position gated
at $g$ is billed $g$ times the per-token FFN cost; attention is always paid
in full. All variants share Eq.~\eqref{eq:gate} and the accounting, so
comparisons isolate the gating signal. We gate the FFN only, never the
attention's keys or values: skipping a token's FFN is safe because downstream
tokens still attend to it, whereas skipping K/V irreversibly deletes
information that later positions may need.

\subsection{Why endogenous signals fail: three diagnostics}
\label{sec:failures}

End-to-end training offers the gate one channel of feedback: the task loss.
\emph{Collapse}: a signal with no difficulty information (attention entropy
\citep{clark2019bert}, $r \approx -0.06$ per token with difficulty) drives
the gate to a constant.
\emph{Inversion}: a ground-truth initialization makes the gate vary, but easy
problems are memorizable, so their cross-entropy gradient is largest and
training allocates them \emph{more} compute (per-problem gate--difficulty
$r = -0.59$). \emph{Vacuity}: a head trained only through the task loss
receives no signal about what difficulty is; the gate stays flat and unstable
across seeds. We report the two iteration-1 quantities as distinct: per-token
entropy--difficulty correlation $r \approx -0.06$, and per-problem
entropy-gate--difficulty $r = 0.23$ (the gate passes the tag embedding through
a bottleneck; the two correlations measure different objects).

\subsection{Explicit difficulty supervision with a budget constraint}
\label{sec:supervised}

Training is two-stage. \textbf{Stage 1} trains the body conventionally with
the gate fixed at $1$ and checkpoints it. \textbf{Stage 2} attaches the
DifficultyHeads and minimizes
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{L} = \mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{CE}}
+ \lambda_{\mathrm{mse}}\,\tfrac{1}{N}\sum_{t}\bigl(\sigma(s_t) - \tau_t\bigr)^2
+ \lambda_{\mathrm{budget}}\,\bigl(\bar{g} - 0.5\bigr)^2,
\label{eq:loss}
\end{equation}
with $\lambda_{\mathrm{mse}} = 5.0$, $\lambda_{\mathrm{budget}} = 0.5$; the
body trains at a near-frozen learning rate ($10^{-2}\times$ the heads').
Stage 1 is shared by every fork of the method (all Stage-2 arms load the same
checkpoint per seed), so method comparisons are paired by body.

\subsection{Measuring the value of compute}
\label{sec:measure}

Where difficulty labels are absent (all natural-language settings), targets
$\tau_t$ are \emph{measured offline} from the Stage-1 checkpoint:
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{score}_t \;=\; -\sum_{\ell} \frac{\partial \mathcal{L}}{\partial
g_{t,\ell}}\Big|_{g=1},
\label{eq:gategrad}
\end{equation}
the marginal loss reduction from one unit of FFN compute at token $t$,
obtained for all tokens and layers in a single forward--backward pass with
gates as differentiable leaves. Scores ${\le}0$ are \emph{irreducible}
(compute does not help there) and receive the floor target $0.1$; positive
scores are quartile-binned to $0.2/0.4/0.6/0.8$. This is the paper's key
conceptual move: tags label \emph{difficulty}; Eq.~\eqref{eq:gategrad}
measures the \emph{value} of compute. The two are different objects, and the
difference is testable: score--loss correlation is near zero in our runs---%
loss says ``hard,'' the gate gradient says ``helpable''---and a token's tag
bin does not constrain its score (a hard bin mixes irreducible tokens with
worth-treating ones). We therefore expect, and report, that compute-value
supervision does \emph{not} order gates by difficulty bins at the testbed
scale: bin-level spread is not its target. The inversion---%
end-to-end training overfeeding easy tokens---is the same phenomenon seen
from the other side: training chases reducibility wherever it lives, but
uncontrollably; the budget term is what makes chasing value safe. A
loss-quantile variant (the proxy-difficulty ablation) bins tokens by
per-token loss; a \emph{shuffled} control permutes targets within each
sequence, testing whether any varying supervision would do.

\paragraph{First-order oracle allocation.} The same measurements give a
reference point: sorting per-(token, layer) scores and filling until the
budget is exhausted is the first-order-optimal allocation under a
\emph{frozen} body, evaluated zero-shot by overriding gates at inference;
it bounds what allocation alone buys without retraining. Trained methods
may exceed it---their bodies adapt to their gates---which we read as
adaptation compounding allocation, not as a paradox.

\subsection{Iso-FLOPs training protocol}
\label{sec:isoflops}

Every run logs cumulative \emph{billed training FLOPs}: the gated billing of
\S\ref{sec:arch} applied to forward, with backward billed at twice forward
(total $3\times$). Methods are compared at matched achieved budgets by
linear interpolation of the evaluation history \emph{within} training
segments only---two-stage runs have a kink at the stage boundary, and budgets
common to all methods are capped at the shortest run's endpoint (no
extrapolation). At matched billed FLOPs, lower-mean-gate methods receive more
optimizer steps per billed dollar; that is the deployment claim under test,
and steps-matched comparisons are reported alongside in
Appendix~\ref{app:stepsmatched}.

\section{Experimental setup}
\label{sec:setup}

\paragraph{Arithmetic.} Two regimes with graded difficulty: \emph{tagged}
([E]/[M]/[H] prefixes; easy 1--2-digit $\pm$, medium 4--5-digit $\pm$, hard
$4{\times}4$, $5{\times}4$ and $6{\times}5$-digit multiplication at scale) and
\emph{mixed} (no tags; operands 1--7 digits; bin = larger operand's digit
count). The hard regime is chosen per scale so the largest model sits inside
a difficulty window: hard-bin accuracy neither saturated nor collapsed
(maintained in $[40\%, 85\%]$ by the calibration probes). Frozen held-out
sets, identical across methods and seeds; accuracy on answer tokens only.
Models: an 11M testbed (the earlier workshop setting, exactly reproduced) and
a 150M scaled model (RoPE \citep{su2024roformer}, flash attention
\citep{dao2022flashattention}, bf16, cosine schedule; identical across
methods), probed healthy (10k steps) and starved (2.5k steps).

\paragraph{Natural language.} Pretrained Pythia-1.4b \citep{biderman2023pythia}
continue-trained on web text (FineWeb-Edu \citep{penedo2024fineweb}, 350M
packed tokens, sequence length 2048, AdamW at
$10^{-5}$ with cosine decay and 2\% warmup, bf16 autocast over fp32
master weights): a dense arm and a token-level-FFN-dropout arm, same data
and step budget, the recipe pair of \S\ref{sec:causal} at language scale.
The oracle machinery of \S\ref{sec:measure} and the code/math domains
(CodeSearchNet \citep{husain2019codesearchnet}, MetaMathQA
\citep{yu2024metamath}) are implemented but the oracle was not run at NL
scale and is not claimed. Metric: full-held-out loss; the rotation arm is
reported at dense deployment (gates off), its pre-registered evaluation
mode.

\paragraph{Methods compared.} \emph{Baseline} (dense; = Stage-1), ours
(\emph{gate-grad} targets; \emph{loss-quantile} ablation), \emph{shuffled}
control, \emph{Fixed-schedule} (static $g{=}0.5$), \emph{MoD} at matched
capacity, and the endogenous
iterations (\emph{entropy}, \emph{random}, \emph{tag-only}) at testbed scale.

\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}

\subsection{Endogenous signals fail; supervision fixes allocation}
\label{sec:failures-results}

The three failure modes are already visible in the 11M testbed runs
(numbers ported verbatim from an earlier workshop version of this work,
whose artifacts predate this repository): entropy $0.232/0.233/0.233$
across E/M/H; tag-init inverted; unsupervised vacuous; supervised spread
$+0.21$, ordering $r = 0.95$ in every seed.

\label{sec:p0}

\paragraph{Pre-registered decision rule.} Written before any shuffled
number existed: the measured-targets claim survives iff gate-grad beats
the shuffled control by ${\ge}1.0$ accuracy point, paired by seed at
matched billed training FLOPs, in all three seeds; no post-hoc
reinterpretation either way.

\paragraph{Outcome: the rule fired against the measured targets.} Paired
gate-grad $-$ shuffled accuracy across seeds: $-0.3$, $-0.1$, and $-0.0$
points (Figure~\ref{fig:content}a).
At testbed scale, \emph{any} varying supervision under the budget
constraint---even targets permuted into noise---recovers the same
accuracy (the seed-paired lines of Figure~\ref{fig:content}a never
separate) while all three dominate a
constant gate trained from scratch by ${\sim}2$ points at the same inference
budget (the budget redistribution this induces is quantified in
Appendix~\ref{app:budget}). Two honest readings follow. First, at $11$M
supervision supplies a dense-then-gate protocol whose accuracy is
insensitive to target content; the tag arm's $+0.21$ ordering spread is
real but not accuracy-load-bearing. Second, the
value-of-compute concept is not refuted but falsifiable at scale: its
per-bin signature must migrate with capability (\S\ref{sec:scale}), and its
natural-language targets must beat shuffled ones precisely where compute
has leverage (\S\ref{sec:nl}). Both tests were pre-registered; neither
favored the measured targets. Gate--score agreement on held-out tokens is
near zero in every seed (Pearson $\le 0.04$, Spearman $\le 0.09$):
gate-grad varies within problems, so per-bin spread
(Figure~\ref{fig:content}b) is not its allocation metric.

Table~\ref{tab:causal}: token-level FFN dropout and random 100-step windows

half-FLOP training buys at convergence.

model holds held-out loss $\ConvRotLoss$ vs dense $\ConvDenseLoss$

Table~\ref{tab:p150}: content-irrelevance replicates at 150M---tag,

Table~\ref{tab:causal} is the decisive test. The starved-150M regime is
where the value field is demonstrably \emph{alive}: the discriminator shows
real ordering structure (waterfill $+$0.30/$+$0.38 over random across two
seeds). If that structure were exploitable, training online against the
measured field should beat random allocation. It does not: online loses to
random windows ($41.7$ vs $44.2$), replicating the 11M causal result at a
second scale, and the static gate collapses outright ($22.4$, held loss
diverging). Figure~\ref{fig:teaser}(b) shows the full trajectory: the
ordering value rises, peaks, and decays to zero as the body converges (the
step-5000 sign flips are seed-inconsistent and pre-registered as noise).
The life cycle and
the causal test together: the field's structure is real, measurable---and
not a training signal.

\subsection{Scale: the effect survives 11M $\to$ 150M}
\label{sec:scale}

\paragraph{Pre-registered readout: value migrates with capability.} An 11M
model finds most hard-bin tokens irreducible; a 400M model would not.
Prediction (written before the probes ran): the hard bin's mean gate-grad
score rises and its fraction of non-positive scores falls toward the
medium bin's, across $42$M $\to$ $150$M $\to$ $400$M. A flat signature at
\$10 of probe cost would have falsified the irreducibility mechanism
cheaply.

\emph{The rule fired against the prediction.} On healthy bodies (42M acc
$0.496$, 150M acc $0.563$), the measured field is flat at convergence:
frac(score${\le}0)\approx 0.51$ across E/M/H bins at both sizes, mean
$|$score$|$ at $10^{-6}$ (coin-flip; Table~\ref{tab:migration})---no
difficulty-bin structure, no migration. The compute-value concept is
therefore parked as a \emph{training-time} signal: the starved-regime
discriminator shows structure where the body is plastic, but neither
converged-body oracle allocation nor online chasing converts it into
accuracy (Figure~\ref{fig:ladder}b).

architecture does not train at 400M, so no frontier claim is made beyond

evidence).

\subsection{Wall-clock realization}
\label{sec:realization}

Billed FLOPs are an accounting device; this section measures what a
realization actually pays. We time full training steps (forward + backward,
$4{\times}2048$ tokens) of Pythia-1.4b under three execution strategies:
dense; the \emph{masked} path our training driver uses (compute every FFN
row, multiply masked tokens by zero); and a \emph{gather-skip} path that
removes masked tokens' FFN rows (index-select on the flattened token
axis). On an A100: dense runs at $\WcDenseTok$ tok/s; masked-at-half runs
at $\WcMaskedHalf\times$ --- zeroing saves nothing --- while gather-skip
reaches $\WcSkipHalf\times$ dense at budget $0.5$, $\WcSkipThreeQ\times$
at $0.75$, and $\WcSkipQuarter\times$ at $0.25$. Two honest readings.
First, the saving is real but sub-linear: FFNs are only a fraction of step
time (attention, optimizer, and memory movement do not shrink), so the
billed $2\times$ arrives as a measured $1.35\times$. Second, the naive
masked path --- what most token-dropping code actually ships --- realizes
\emph{nothing}; the gather is the whole result.

\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

\paragraph{Implications.} The recipe---train dense, measure, impose---needs
no endogenous-signal search and plugs into serving stacks that already
produce per-token difficulty estimates (speculative-decoding acceptance
\citep{leviathan2023speculative,chen2023speculative}).
\paragraph{Which chasers does ``not trainable'' rule out?} The causal arms
test the natural first-order chaser: measure $\partial\mathcal{L}/\partial
g$ on the current body, allocate hard against it, refresh every 100 steps.
Stronger search over the same objective (bandits, RL, second-order
corrections) faces the same structural problem: the scores are
a property of the current body; training under them changes the body; and
the ordering value decays as the body absorbs compute---the chaser
destroys the signal it chases. The frozen-body oracle
(Fig.~\ref{fig:ladder}) caps what \emph{any} ordering could buy---below
not budgeting at all in every regime measured. We read ``not trainable''
as a property of gradient-defined value fields, not an impossibility
proof for all conceivable allocators.
\paragraph{When static schedules suffice.} A constant $g{=}0.5$ from
scratch collapses at every scale tested (11M: $31.8\%$; starved-150M:
$22.4\%$, diverging), but at deployment the static choice is trivially
optimal---the converged field is flat, so full compute everywhere is the
right static schedule. Between those endpoints, static schedules suffice
exactly when the loss does not benefit from intermittent compute;
MetaMathQA (\S\ref{sec:nl}) is the counter-case.
\paragraph{Limitations.} The 1.4B headline rests on three seeds
(per-seed gaps \NlRotGapMin--\NlRotGapMax\%; the third attempt
initially failed twice to host failures); code and math are single-seed. Arithmetic difficulty is
operand-size-defined; NL bins inherit the reference model's notion of
difficulty. The two-stage body is near-frozen (App.~\ref{app:ablations});
billed FLOPs bridge to wall-clock in \S\ref{sec:realization}; quartile
\emph{form} is hand-set, its content measured.

\section{Ablations}
\label{app:ablations}

\paragraph{Full-unfreeze Stage-2.} The default protocol near-freezes the
body ($10^{-2}\times$ the head learning rate). Unfreezing it fully on the
web-text fork (seed 1, 2000 steps): held loss $2.6054$ vs.\ the
near-frozen $2.7077$ and dense $2.5322$ --- the body adapting to its
gates recovers $58\%$ of the remaining gap, consistent with the
main-text reading that trained methods can exceed frozen-body allocation
by adaptation, not by better ordering (gate profiles stay flat:
$[0.33, 0.34, 0.35, 0.35]$).
\paragraph{Per-layer target freedom.} Implemented end-to-end (per-layer
oracle scores, per-layer quartile bins, per-layer gate targets); the
single run hung on our 40GB budget and was abandoned under the results
freeze --- reported as not run, left to future work. The summed-score
protocol of the main text is unaffected.
\paragraph{Shuffled control at all scales.} 11M: Table~\ref{tab:p0};
150M: Table~\ref{tab:p150}; 1.4B web text: \S\ref{sec:nl} --- the paired
gate-grad $-$ shuffled difference is $0.0008$ at language scale,
replicating the testbed result.

\section{Experimental details}
\label{app:details}

\paragraph{Arithmetic.} Vocabulary of 22 digit/operator tokens; frozen
held-out sets of 256 sequences per split, identical across arms and
seeds; accuracy on answer tokens only. 11M: $d{=}384$, 6 layers, 6 heads,
$d_{ff}{=}1536$, sequence length 256, batch 32, LR $3{\times}10^{-4}$
(the raw recipe trains stably at this scale). 150M: $d{=}1024$, 12
layers, RoPE, SDPA attention, sequence length 512, batch 32, validated
recipe: LR $10^{-4}$, cosine decay to $0.1\times$, 2\% warmup, weight
decay $0.1$. The 11M causal test runs 3000 steps; the 150M grid 5000;
the starved regime 2500; the 150M convergence pair 10000.

\paragraph{Natural language.} Pythia-1.4b continue-trained on FineWeb-Edu
(350M tokens packed to 2049-token rows, 167{,}398 train / 3{,}416 held-out
sequences), AdamW $10^{-5}$, cosine to $0.1\times$ with 2\% warmup,
bf16 autocast over fp32 master weights, effective batch 8 sequences via
4-sequence micro-batches $\times$ gradient accumulation 2, 3000 steps,
evaluations every 250 steps on a fixed 32-sequence subsample with a full
held-out evaluation at the final step. Code domain: CodeSearchNet python
(function docstring + body); math domain: MetaMathQA (question +
response). The per-layer oracle stores per-(token, layer) scores in fp32
(negative partial losses) and quartile-bins each layer's positive scores.

\paragraph{Wall-clock.} Measured on an A100: full forward+backward steps
at batch $4{\times}2048$, 3 warmup + 10 timed iterations, synchronized;
the gather-skip path index-selects active token rows before each FFN and
index-copies results back.
